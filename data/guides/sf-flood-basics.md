## Free sandbags
San Francisco residents and businesses can get up to 10 free sandbags at the Public Works Operations Yard. Enter at the Kansas Street and Marin Street gate. Open Monday to Saturday, 8 AM to 2 PM, year-round, while supplies last. Bring proof of address and be ready to load the bags into your vehicle yourself. Source: https://www.sfpublicworks.org/services/sandbags

## Sandbag Saturdays 2026
Public Works runs neighborhood sandbag giveaways from 8 AM to 2 PM on November 7, 14 and 21 and December 5, at sites including Marina Green, Lake Merced, Ocean Beach, Sherman Street and the Fire Training Center. Limited to 10 sandbags per address; bring proof that you live or run a business in San Francisco. Source: https://www.sfpublicworks.org/services/sandbags

## The 100-Year Storm Flood Risk Map
SFPUC's 100-Year Storm Flood Risk Map shows parcels that are highly likely to see deep and contiguous flooding in a 100-year storm: at least 6 inches deep and covering about half an average city block. A 100-year storm has a 1% chance of happening in any year. The map covers stormwater runoff only, not flooding from the Bay, the ocean or lakes. It was last updated in December 2024. San Francisco law requires sellers and landlords to tell buyers and tenants if a property is in the flood risk zone. To ask about a property, contact RainReadySF@sfwater.org or (415) 554-3289. Source: https://www.sfpuc.gov/learning/emergency-preparedness/flood-maps

## Floodwater Grant (up to $100,000)
SFPUC's Floodwater Grant gives eligible property owners up to $100,000 for flood-protection measures, such as a regraded driveway with raised planters that work as flood barriers, or deployable barriers for driveways and doorways. Owners may qualify if they had rain-related flooding from the street or sidewalk, or from plumbing fixtures connected to the City sewer system. Contact FloodwaterGrants@sfwater.org or (415) 523-4412. Source: https://www.sfpuc.gov/floodwatergrant

## Flood insurance
San Francisco takes part in the National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP), so homeowners, renters and businesses can buy federally backed flood insurance. Standard homeowner and business policies usually do not cover flood damage. NFIP policies normally have a 30-day waiting period before coverage starts, so buy before the rainy season. Source: https://www.sf.gov/san-francisco-floodplain-administration

## Adopt a Drain
San Francisco has about 25,000 storm drains (catch basins). Leaves clog them and cause street flooding. Volunteers can adopt a drain, name it, and get an email before big storms. To clean a drain safely: wear a reflective vest, wear gloves, watch for cars and stay close to the sidewalk, use a rake, broom or shovel (not your hands), put leaves in the green compost bin and recyclables in the blue bin. Source: https://adoptadrain.sfwater.org

## Report flooding, sewer backups or fallen trees
Report street flooding, clogged storm drains, sewer backups and fallen trees to 311: call 311, use the SF311 app, or go to sf311.org. 311 has translation in many languages. If water is entering your home and someone is in danger, or a downed power line is involved, call 911. Source: https://sf311.org

## Official alerts
AlertSF is San Francisco's official emergency text alert system. Text your ZIP code to 888-777 to sign up, or go to alertsf.org. Wireless Emergency Alerts also reach phones without sign-up. Source: https://www.sf.gov/alertsf

## Flooded streets: turn around, don't drown
Never walk or drive through flood water. Six inches of moving water can knock a person down, and about a foot of moving water can carry away a small car. Underpasses can fill quickly. Source: https://www.weather.gov/safety/flood-turn-around-dont-drown
